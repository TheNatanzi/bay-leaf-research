# Overnight stays and hidden obligations

The **Overnight stays** column group separates sleeping capacity, included guests/nights, required accommodation units, minimum nights, late-night requirements, and the payer. Each value links to its source and conditions in the full breakdown. Missing values stay Unknown.

**One unit-night is one accommodation unit for one night.** Forty units for two nights is 80 unit-nights, not 80 rooms or guests. A tent, cottage, bedroom and hotel room are different inventory types; beds do not establish legal sleeping occupancy.

## Compare three different money figures

1. **Contractual obligation:** everything someone must commit to, including required lodging, minimum nights, taxes, resort/service/cleaning fees and attrition.
2. **Host net cost:** the host's obligation minus actual, contractually confirmed guest payments that reduce that obligation. Hoping guests will reimburse you does not qualify.
3. **Guest burden:** what guests must pay, required nights and whether attending the wedding requires booking that accommodation.

The shared [coffee worksheet](coffee-worksheet.html) lets you agree on these assumptions. In the atlas scenario, a known mandatory buyout prevents a complete total until it is explicitly accounted for. A fixed-charge lodging assumption must cover all lodging charges and liability. Avoid applying tax/service again to a lodging amount that already includes them; choose a nonoverlapping or custom charge base. No speculative guest reimbursement is deducted.

Other researched obligations include minimum rental/guard hours, extra security, insurance, full-service planner requirements, shuttles, tent/rain contingencies, kitchen limitations, outside-vendor approval, renter cleanup, trash overages and cancellation penalties. See individual source scopes; a policy about a public food sale is not automatically a private-wedding rule.
