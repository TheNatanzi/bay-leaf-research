# Public concession case study: Ardenwood

**Historical business evidence; current event concession discontinued.**

The [2024 district RFP](https://www.ebparks.org/sites/default/files/Ardenwood-Cafe-and-Event-ConcessionRFP.pdf) proposed a three-year concession with an optional seven-year extension; no leasehold ownership. Its published historical sales table separates wedding site rent from the broader cafe/event operation:

| Sales year | Total reported sales | Wedding site rent sales |
|---|---:|---:|
|2017|$710,883.07|$115,900.00|
|2018|$837,101.15|$117,475.00|
|2019|$1,002,270.65|$140,800.00|
|2020|$126,673.58|$28,553.74|
|2021|$779,480.07|$124,479.20|
|2022|$675,603.72|$81,000.00|

These are reported sales, **not profit, NOI, valuation, or current forecasts**. No event counts or expense verification establish margin. Do not apply an earnings multiple to gross sales.

The [newer food-only RFP](https://www.ebparks.org/sites/default/files/rfp-ardenwood-cafe-concession.pdf) had a December 1, 2025 deadline, not a current open invitation. In the [January 14, 2026 operations meeting](https://ebparks.new.swagit.com/videos/372115), district staff describe eagle-nesting buffers, cancellations/relocations, and ending the incompatible event concession; the former operator moved to Little Hills. Treat automated meeting transcription as a review aid; verify exact operational status before decisions.

**Bay Leaf lesson (our inference):** habitat restrictions, seasonal event rights and concession tenure belong in the business feasibility model before relying on inexpensive public land. Separate venue-only revenue from catering, alcohol and other sales. A beautiful site and historical midnight access do not establish viable current operating rights.
