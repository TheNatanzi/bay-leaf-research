# What a planner needs from every venue

Original Bay Leaf synthesis • 194 research fields in 18 categories • reviewed October 10, 2026 (Pacific).

Start with suitability, then investigate logistics and complete cost. Every venue gets the same framework; irrelevant fields become **Not applicable** with a reason. Unknown never means allowed, included, free or zero. This is a proposed research standard, not verified answers for all venues.

## The answer format

For each field record: **answer • evidence/status • source URL or written document • date checked • package/date/guest-count assumptions • unresolved question • person responsible • next action/deadline**. Keep quotes and private correspondence in shared private context, not the public website.

Evidence labels: **Authority/document verified; Operator published; Directory reported; Observed on visit; Assumption/calculated; Unknown — inquire; Conflicting; Not applicable; Prohibited/closed**. Written sales answers do not establish legal occupancy. A permission can have conditions; preserve them.

## Research stages

1. Broad inventory: capture leads even when wedding permission is unknown. Do not silently discard unconventional spaces.
2. Uniform inquiry: request terms and itemized prices before tours. Use guest-count scenarios for research without pretending they are booked plans.
3. Joint visit: walk the guest route, supplier route and weather backup with floor plans; check equipment and responsibility gaps.
4. Contract validation: reconcile every promised inclusion and exception with the final terms and relevant approvals.
5. Business review: separately examine repeat use, improvements, operating effort and financial feasibility.

The public comparison should show the useful summary. Expandable details hold the full checklist rather than forcing every question into a very wide table. Filter by status, county, venue type, capacity basis, price basis and restrictions; sort unknowns last.

## Cost per head: compare the same thing

Maintain three distinct measures: **venue-only ÷ guests**, **food/bar package per guest**, and **required event total ÷ guests**. The last is the useful comparison only when all mandatory components are known. It does not include optional photographer, attire or other whole-wedding spending unless explicitly selected.

For a comparable scenario, calculate the applicable venue/space fee + contracted food/bar spend after its minimum + mandatory staffing/rentals + compulsory access, cleaning, transport and permit costs + service charges and tax on their documented bases. Divide by the stated guest count. Count included items once. Keep refundable deposits as cash needed, not an event expense. Avoid adding a package rate to a minimum if the minimum already covers that package.

Record children/vendor-meal treatment and date/season. A “from” price has no known upper bound. If required charges are missing, show a **partial known subtotal**, not an all-in range. Do not manufacture a maximum from an optimistic estimate. Capital required for the business remains a research output, not an imposed budget limit.

## What is especially easy to miss

- A wet-weather room may exist yet be unreserved or too small for dinner plus dancing.
- An item can be available on site without being included; quantities and setup labor matter.
- Vendor lists can be optional, mandatory, or subject to an exception fee.
- A five-hour reception does not identify music cutoff, bar closure, or final departure.
- A high maximum may describe standing use, not the intended seating/dancing layout.
- “Hands-off ownership” still requires named responsibility for safety, guest issues, load-in, cleaning and lockup. Model paid labor and owner hours separately.

## California and Bay Area checks

California ABC states that Type 58 catered alcohol events require event authorization and ordinarily have a 36-event annual limit at a location, with an exception process. This is not a general cap on weddings and does not determine every BYOB arrangement. Treat the alcohol operating route and any exception as a business due-diligence question. [ABC official FAQ](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit/).

Accessibility is a continuous experience through arrival, rooms, seating and restrooms. “Historic” or “grandfathered” is not an automatic ADA exemption. [DOJ small-business guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/title-iii-primer/).

Do not convert a one-time special-event rental into assumed permission for weekly commercial weddings. Confirm actual jurisdiction, land-use entitlement, permit conditions, occupancy, fire access, noise and food/wastewater requirements for the property. The reviewed Sonoma staff report contains proposed changes, not proof of final adoption; numerical thresholds remain unverified. [Sonoma source](https://sonoma-county.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=3795E6F1-7701-4414-AB81-C8BB81143BC1&ID=7768001).

## Venue age and operating history

Record construction year, wedding-operation opening date, current-operator start date and operating duration separately. For multiple structures, identify which building the year describes. Track conversions, rebuilding and interruptions. A historic lodge date does not prove the current reception building was built that year. Exact opening dates allow elapsed years; year-only records produce an approximate duration with an as-of date. Unknown stays Unknown — inquire.

## Value assessment



| Field | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Parcel/APN, area, zoning, buildings | Confirm the exact asset; a venue address may cover multiple parcels or a leased portion |
| Owner versus operator | Determine what could actually be purchased or leased |
| Last recorded sale and date | Historical transaction, not today's market value; inspect whether it covered these assets |
| Current asking price and scope | Seller expectation; may include land, business, equipment or several properties |
| Assessed land/improvement value and tax year | Tax basis, kept separate from market value |
| Comparable closed sales | Match use, location, condition, acreage/building area and transferable approvals |
| Property estimate low/high | Only when comps or another documented method support the range |
| Business estimate low/high | Requires verified earnings/assets/contracts and valuation assumptions |
| Rent/lease term and assignment | For a tenant operation, land value is not automatically part of the business acquisition |
| Valuation date/method/confidence | Make the estimate reproducible and label evidence gaps |

California tax assessments and current market values can differ because of assessment rules. Do not use the assessed number as a purchase-price estimate. [California BOE explanation](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta25023.pdf).
