# Venue value assessment

Track **real estate**, **operating business**, and **lease/concession rights** separately. No defensible valuation range has yet been established for the two initial venue records. Unknowns remain explicit; not every venue is for sale.

## Can Zillow help?

Zillow says Zestimates apply to residential properties, excluding commercial property and vacant land. A residential estimate may inform the house component of an estate, but cannot value its wedding operation, event permissions, extra parcels or special-purpose structures. A Zestimate is not an appraisal. [Zillow FAQ](https://www.zillow.com/zestimate/).

## Evidence to collect for each venue

| Field | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Parcel/APN, area, zoning, buildings | Confirm the exact asset; a venue address may cover multiple parcels or a leased portion |
| Owner versus operator | Determine what could actually be purchased or leased |
| Last recorded sale and date | Historical transaction, not today's market value; inspect whether it covered these assets |
| Current asking price and scope | Seller expectation; may include land, business, equipment or several properties |
| Assessed land/improvement value and tax year | Tax basis, kept separate from market value |
| Comparable closed sales | Match use, location, condition, acreage/building area and transferable approvals |
| Property estimate low/high | Only when comps or another documented method support the range |
| Business estimate low/high | Requires verified earnings/assets/contracts and valuation assumptions |
| Rent/lease term and assignment | For a tenant operation, land value is not automatically part of the business acquisition |
| Valuation date/method/confidence | Make the estimate reproducible and label evidence gaps |

California tax assessments and current market values can differ because of assessment rules. Do not use the assessed number as a purchase-price estimate. [California BOE explanation](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta25023.pdf).

## How the research will work

Use assessor/recorder records for parcel and historical facts; [LoopNet's commercial valuation guidance](https://www.loopnet.com/cre-explained/tools/commercial-property-valuation/) and [Crexi commercial listings](https://www.crexi.com/) for relevant sale/lease evidence. Listings are asking prices; sold comps need actual closing dates and asset scope. Restricted or paywalled evidence is recorded as unavailable, not guessed.

Sales comparisons, an income approach and a replacement-cost approach answer different questions. A property's income valuation uses appropriate real-estate operating income and market-supported assumptions, not gross wedding revenue. Unique buildings may have weak comps. The operating business requires separate verification of earnings after normalizing owner labor, market rent, maintenance and one-time costs. A generic earnings multiple is not sufficient evidence for a competitor's valuation.

For an acquisition, model equipment, transferable contracts/approvals, customer deposits and obligations, lease rights, necessary improvements and working capital. Do not add a property value to an earnings-based business value if the same economic benefit is counted twice. Buying an established operation and buying its property are distinct transactions.

Public parks and government sites may offer rentals or concession/lease rights rather than a purchasable venue. Record Not applicable where ownership rules make an acquisition-value field irrelevant, with a reason; replacement cost is a different question.

This is an evidence-collection method. Preliminary ranges, when supported, remain research estimates with dates and assumptions; financing/purchase decisions need property-specific appraisal and business due diligence.
