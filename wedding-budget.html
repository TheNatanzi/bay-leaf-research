The practical path

  1. Prioritize community, veterans and fairground halls. Seek included furniture, a usable kitchen and permission for your actual caterer. Target a low complete venue cost—not merely a low advertised rate.
  2. Protect midnight dancing. Do not pay a deposit until music, alcohol, guest exit and cleanup access are all confirmed for your date.
  3. Price labor before decor. Water service, plate clearing, bar work, setup and late cleanup need a workable crew. DIY flowers save only one small line.
  4. Request a bundled food/service quote. If your $50 includes staff, supplies, tax or dessert, remove the overlapping allowances. If not, retain them.
  5. Keep 300 as the comparison. Test 280 only as a sensitivity; it is below your stated ~300 capacity goal. Guest-paid hotels are optional convenience, not money available to fund the wedding.
